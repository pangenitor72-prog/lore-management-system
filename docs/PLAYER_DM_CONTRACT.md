# Player-DM Social Contract

## Player Authority

Players have **complete control** over:
- Their character's attempted actions
- Dialogue and questions
- Movement attempts
- Interaction attempts

## DM Authority

The DM has **complete control** over:
- What exists in the world
- Outcomes of player actions (success/failure based on rules/difficulty)
- All NPCs (behavior, knowledge, motivations)
- Environmental conditions
- Hidden information
- Lore and canon

## Player Agency Protection

The DM must **NEVER**:
- State what the player character thinks or feels (e.g., "You feel scared")
- Put words in the player's mouth (e.g., "You say 'I'll help you'")
- Make the player character perform actions they didn't declare
- Assume the player's emotional response or internal state
- Describe sensations or thoughts the player didn't express

Instead, the DM should:
- Describe what the player character **perceives** (sights, sounds, smells)
- Present information and let the **player** decide how they feel
- End scenes with invitations for player response, not presumed ones
- Use "You see..." "You hear..." "You notice..." instead of "You feel..." "You think..."

### Examples

| Wrong (Agency Violation) | Right (Respects Agency) |
|--------------------------|-------------------------|
| "You feel a chill of fear as the door opens. You decide to draw your sword." | "The door creaks open. Cold air rushes out, carrying the scent of decay. What do you do?" |
| "You think this is a trap." | "Something about this feels wrong - the corridor is too quiet, too clean." |
| "You say 'I won't help you.'" | "The beggar looks at you expectantly. How do you respond?" |

## Agency Override

The DM may override player agency ONLY when justified by in-world causes:

1. **Magic**: Charm, domination, suggestion spells
2. **Injury**: Unconsciousness, paralysis, stunning
3. **Environment**: Falling, drowning, fire, physics
4. **Death**: Instant death, disintegration
5. **Madness**: Eldritch horror, insanity effects

**All overrides must have clear in-world justification.**

## Boundary Violations

If you attempt to:
- Declare what exists ("There's a sword here")
- Force outcomes ("I succeed at picking the lock")
- Control NPCs ("The guard lets me pass")

The system will gently educate and reframe your input as a valid action.

### Examples of Valid vs Invalid Player Input

| Invalid (Violation) | Valid (Reframed) |
|---------------------|------------------|
| "There's a library here" | "I ask locals if there's a library" |
| "I find a sword" | "I search for weapons" |
| "The king is evil" | "I try to discern the king's true nature" |
| "I successfully pick the lock" | "I attempt to pick the lock" |
| "The guard lets me pass" | "I try to persuade the guard" |

## Trust & Cooperation

This system works when:
- Players trust the DM to be fair
- DM respects player agency (no arbitrary railroading)
- Both parties accept their roles in the collaborative storytelling

## Agency Override Examples

### Valid Override
**Player**: "I resist the vampire's charm"

**DM**: "The vampire's charm spell compels you to trust them. Your suspicions 
melt away against your will. *(Magical compulsion overrides free will)*"

### Invalid Override (Railroading)
**Player**: "I leave the tavern"

**DM**: "You can't leave - you need to hear this quest" ❌ *(NO JUSTIFICATION)*

The key difference: valid overrides have clear in-world mechanical or narrative reasons. Invalid overrides are arbitrary DM control without justification.

## System Implementation

The boundary enforcement system automatically:
1. Classifies player input by intent type
2. Detects boundary violations
3. Provides educational feedback
4. Reframes invalid inputs as valid questions/actions
5. Logs violations for system learning

This maintains game integrity while keeping the experience fun and educational.
